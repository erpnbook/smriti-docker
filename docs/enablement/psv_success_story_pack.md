## Case Study 1: StepFit Shoes (Footwear Brand Network)

### Executive Summary
StepFit Shoes, a prominent regional footwear brand, operates through a network of 45 distributors and franchise stores in Western India. Before implementing SMRITI PSV, the brand struggled with stockouts on standard size curves and excess capital locked in extreme sizes.

---

### The Challenge (Before SMRITI PSV)
*   **Blind Channel Operations**: StepFit only tracked dispatches from its Pune central factory. It had zero visibility into distributor inventory.
*   **The Size Curve Trap**: Distributors frequently ran out of fast-moving Sizes 7 and 8 sneakers, losing sales. Meanwhile, Sizes 6 and 11 accumulated in depots.
*   **Capital Locked**: Stagnant stock sitting at distributor depots locked up substantial capital, leading to low turnover rates.
*   **Baseline Metrics**:
    *   Annual Network Sales: **₹5,00,00,000 (₹5 Cr)**
    *   Out-of-Stock Rate (Bestsellers): **18%**
    *   Capital Locked in Dead Stock: **₹15,00,000**
    *   Average Distributor ROI: **20%**

---

### The Solution
StepFit implemented SMRITI **Party Stock Visibility (PSV)** to build an integrated Inventory Visibility Network:
1.  **Daily Sales Uploads**: Distributors integrated their POS checkout logs, giving StepFit real-time secondary sales visibility.
2.  **WOC Monitoring**: The system computed Weeks of Cover (WOC) dynamically, warning planners 3 weeks before size stockouts occurred.
3.  **Network Stock Transfers**: StepFit balanced regional stock by transferring size surpluses from overstocked depots directly to depots facing stockouts.

---

### The Results (After SMRITI PSV)
Within 90 days of rollout, StepFit achieved significant operational improvements:
*   **Bestseller Stockouts**: Reduced from **18% to 6%** through automated reorder buffer planning.
*   **Incremental Sales**: Recovered lost size sales, boosting annual revenue to **₹6,50,00,000 (₹6.5 Cr)**—a **30% sales growth** without expanding manufacturing capacity.
*   **Capital Freed**: Cleared stagnant stock using inter-store transfers, releasing **₹6,00,000** in distributor working capital.
*   **Distributor ROI**: Reinvesting the freed capital into high-velocity variants boosted average distributor ROI from **20% to 26%**.

    > [!NOTE]
    > **Distributor ROI Shift Derivation (CFO Note)**:
    > *   **Formula**: $\text{Distributor ROI \%} = \left(\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Invested Capital (Inventory Value)}}\right) \times 100$
    > *   **Before SMRITI**: A distributor holding ₹1,00,00,000 in average inventory generates ₹20,00,000 in annual net profit $\rightarrow \text{ROI} = 20\%$.
    > *   **After SMRITI**: Reinvesting freed capital into high-velocity variant curves reduces dead stock holdings and captures lost sales, boosting annual net profit by ₹6,00,000 to ₹26,00,000 on the same capital base $\rightarrow \text{ROI} = \left(\frac{26,00,000}{1,00,00,000}\right) \times 100 = 26\%$.
